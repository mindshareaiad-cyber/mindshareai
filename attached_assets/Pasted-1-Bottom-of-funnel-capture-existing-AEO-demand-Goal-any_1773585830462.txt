1. Bottom-of-funnel: capture existing AEO demand
Goal: anyone already searching “AEO tools”, “AI share of voice”, “[competitor] alternative” finds you and sees a clear, opinionated comparison.

1.1. Pick targets and pages
Choose 5–7 initial competitors from current AEO lists (Relixir, HubSpot AEO Grader, Scrunch, SE Visible, Goodie/Evertune-style tools, etc.).

Create:

1 “Best AEO tools for B2B SaaS in 2026” page.

5–7 “Mindshare AI vs [Competitor]” pages.

1.2. Build a comparison template once
In your CMS (or as a React layout), define a reusable block with:

Hero: H1 + 2–3 sentence summary + CTA button (“Run a free AI visibility check”).

“Who each is best for” bullets.

Feature comparison table.

Sections:

“AI coverage & share of voice metrics”

“Use cases & ideal customer profile”

“Pricing & onboarding”

“When to choose Mindshare AI vs [Competitor]”

CTA sections mid-page and bottom.

Then each new compare page is just “fill in variables and text,” not custom design.

1.3. Gather competitor facts in 30–60 minutes each
Skim their homepage, pricing page and 1–2 product pages.

Note down:

Engines they claim to track (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, AI Overviews, etc.).

Core metrics (share of voice, visibility index, prompt coverage, etc.).

Who they say they’re for (enterprise, ecommerce, SaaS, agencies).

Automation / GEO vs pure tracking.

Pricing level (free tool, SMB, enterprise).

Drop those into a small internal spreadsheet so you can reuse for the roundup page.

1.4. Write the “vs” page content
For each competitor:

Intro:

2–3 sentences stating: both solve AEO/AI visibility; Mindshare is for X, competitor is better for Y.

Feature table (like we sketched) using your real features vs their public ones.

3 sections of 2–4 sentences each going into:

Coverage & metrics.

Workflow & ICP fit.

Pricing / complexity trade-offs.

Clear “Choose Mindshare AI if…” and “Choose [Competitor] if…” bullets, being honest (e.g. “If you’re an enterprise with in‑house content ops, [Competitor]’s automation might be better.”).

1.5. Ship “Best AEO tools for B2B SaaS”
List 6–10 tools from current “best AEO tools” posts.

Put Mindshare first, but give each tool a concise, fair description and “best for” line.

Add a comparison table row per tool: coverage, best for, pricing tier.

Angle it to SaaS:

Call out tools that skew to ecommerce / enterprise vs those that fit lean SaaS teams.

1.6. Add on-page SEO basics
Use titles/meta that include “vs”, “[tool] alternative”, “AEO tools for SaaS”.

Drop internal links to:

This roundup page.

Your main “What is AEO / AI share of voice?” explainer.

2. Mid-funnel: ship free, ungated tools
Goal: people with medium intent can use Mindshare without commitment and see value inside 60 seconds.

2.1. Define 1–2 v1 tools
If I were you, I’d start with:

Free “AI share of voice check”:

Inputs: brand + 1–2 competitors + 3–5 prompts or a category (e.g. “email marketing software”).

Output: simple visual or table showing how often each brand appears in AI answers for that set.

“Prompt coverage explorer”:

Input: niche or category.

Output: top X answer-intent questions and whether the brand appears.

You can fake some complexity behind the scenes at first (e.g. run jobs manually) as long as the front-end feels instant.

2.2. Implement as ungated flows
On the site:

“No login required” forms.

Show a partial result instantly; gate export / save / scheduling behind signup.

For v1:

Run the actual checks in your admin or via a simple internal script; email them the full report if needed (people don’t care how the sausage is made).

Add a small watermark or line: “Report created with Mindshare AI” and a shareable link.

2.3. Wire into your onboarding
After someone signs up:

Pre-fill a report for their brand and show it in the dashboard first.

Use one email:

Subject: “We ran your AI visibility check for you.”

Body: screenshot of a key gap and link to the full report with a clear next step (e.g. “Claim answer ownership on these 3 prompts”).

3. Top-of-funnel: programmatic “how to win answers” content
Goal: long-tail traffic + LLM citations for thousands of “how do I show up in AI answers for X?” queries.

3.1. Create a “prompt x engine x niche” schema
Define a simple config:

Niche (e.g. “email marketing software”, “SaaS analytics tools”).

Funnel stage (awareness/consideration/comparison).

Engine (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, AI Overviews).

Question pattern (e.g. “best [niche] for startups”, “top [niche] tools”, “what is [niche]”).

From that schema, define 50–200 initial article topics.

3.2. Build a generic article template
For each topic, a page like:

H1: “How to Rank for ‘best [niche] tools’ in ChatGPT & Perplexity in 2026”

Sections:

“How AI answer engines choose which brands to show” (short, references AEO best practices).

“What ‘winning’ this prompt looks like” (describe ideal answer mentioning you).

“Checklist to rank for this question” (schema, answer-first content, authority).

“See how your brand performs today” (embed or screenshot a Mindshare report).

CTA: “Run this exact prompt set in Mindshare AI for your brand, free.”

Generate most of the content using your schema template + light manual editing.

3.3. Link these pages everywhere
From each relevant blog article and compare page, link out to a small set of these “how to win [prompt]” guides.

Use some as landing pages for very narrow paid keywords (e.g. “rank in Perplexity answers”).

4. Do things that don’t scale: AEO teardown campaign
Goal: hand-to-hand wins with your exact ICP to get first 20–50 real users.

4.1. Craft the offer
“I’ll run a free AI answer engine visibility teardown for 20 SaaS products. Drop your homepage and main category, I’ll send you a 2‑page report showing where you show up (or don’t) in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude.”

4.2. Post it in the right places
/r/SaaS, /r/startups, /r/digital_marketing, Indie Hackers, maybe a couple of niche Slack/Discord groups.

Your LinkedIn feed and comments under relevant AEO/SEO posts.

4.3. Deliver the teardown via Mindshare
For each accepted site:

Run them through your internal Mindshare workflow.

Create:

1 screenshot of an overview SOV dashboard.

1 screenshot of prompt-level gaps.

Record a 3–5 min Loom walking through:

Where they appear.

Their biggest missed prompts.

2–3 actions they could take.

Then:

Email + DM:

Link to the Loom.

Link to a Mindshare workspace pre-populated with their brand.

Soft ask: “If this was useful, can I quote you or grab a 1–2 sentence testimonial? If you’re open to trying the paid plan, I’ll set you up on a 1‑month trial.”

5. Be everywhere your buyers are
Goal: turn yourself into “the AEO + AI visibility guy” for SaaS/growth people.

5.1. LinkedIn cadence
3–5 posts per week for 4–6 weeks:

1 teardown thread a week (anonymised) showing a before/after of AI visibility.

1 “AEO concept” post (e.g. answer-first structure, schema, entity consistency) tied to how Mindshare helps track it.

1 “build in public” product update (e.g. “we just added Gemini coverage; here’s what we found across 100 SaaS brands”).

Each post: one visual (chart/screenshot), one lesson, one soft CTA.

5.2. Guest posts / appearances
Pitch 1–2 AEO thought pieces to places already talking about AEO tools and trends.

Angle: “What we learned running AI visibility checks for 100 SaaS brands.”

Link back to your free tools and teardown offer.

6. Order of operations (what I’d do week by week)
If I were in your shoes with existing dev chops:

Week 1:

Build comparison page template and ship:

Mindshare vs Relixir, vs HubSpot AEO Grader, plus the “Best AEO tools for B2B SaaS” article.

Add a basic “AI visibility check” landing form (even if fulfilment is manual).

Week 2:

Implement v1 of the free SOV checker (front-end + manual or semi-automated backend).

Launch teardown offer on 2–3 communities and on LinkedIn.

Week 3:

Turn 3–5 best teardowns into anonymised case-study style posts and 3–5 “how to win [prompt]” pages.

Ship 2–3 more compare pages for additional tools.

Week 4–6:

Tighten onboarding around that free SOV report.

Keep doing 5–10 teardowns/week until you hit ~100 total teardowns and 20–30 paying users.
